What it does

The demo above is a rough browser copy of the look. The real app runs natively on Windows (written in Rust), follows your monitor's refresh rate, and works with a controller.

Eleven consoles

Xbox 360, Xbox, PS3, PS2, PS1, PSP, Switch, GameCube, N64, DS and 3DS. Each console gets its own channel.

Smooth handoff

The game tile zooms in, the emulator opens fullscreen, and the dashboard waits. Press Guide (or hold Back + Start) to close the game and slide back in.

Uses your emulators

Point it at Xenia, RPCS3, PCSX2, Dolphin, PPSSPP, DuckStation, Eden, Ryujinx, melonDS, Azahar, xemu… Emulators next to the launcher are found automatically.

Built-in cores, only if you ask

No emulator for a console? You can download a libretro core in one step. Nothing is downloaded unless you choose to.

Cover art, automatically

Covers are found by disc ID or serial where possible, by name otherwise. Don't like a cover? Pick another one.

RetroAchievements

Sign in with your normal username and password. Your points and per-game progress show on the dashboard.

Emulator settings in one place

Change resolution, aspect ratio and VSync for PCSX2, Dolphin, DuckStation, PPSSPP, Xenia and xemu from the dashboard. The original settings file is backed up first.

Gamerpics & themes

18 built-in gamerpics, your own pictures with a move-and-zoom crop, and five colour themes that fade into each other.

Motion that feels right

Spring physics everywhere, an intro fly-in, and pages that zoom open the way a console menu does.

Inside the app

Real screenshots. Everything can be done with a controller, a keyboard or a mouse.
